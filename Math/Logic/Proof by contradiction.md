### Definition

To prove a statement P, you assume the opposite $\neg P$ and show that this assumption leads to a contradiction.

When a contradiction appears, it proves that the assumption $\neg P$ must be false, therefore P must be true

##### Structure

To prove P:

1. Assume $\neg P$
2. Derive a contraction
3. Conclude that P is true


#### Example

Statement to prove
"There is no smallest positive rational number".

Let P= “there is no smallest positive rational number".

**Proof by contradicition:**

1. Assume the opposite:
		Suppose there is a smallest positive rational number. Call it r.
		
2. Consider r/2; it is also rational and positive, and r/2 < r

3. This contradicts the assumption that r was the smallest positive rational.
4. Therefore, the assumption is false -> the original statement is true.
